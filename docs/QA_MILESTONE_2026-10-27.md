## Working development capabilities

- Embedded Before/After video and separately identified audio playback.
- Preview-first approval and advancement without automatic Filmora navigation.
- Searchable history, revisiting reviews, source-ordered Previous/Next,
  independent pane scrolling and persistent session/draft state.
- Supported preview-only audio treatments: unchanged, delete tail, outgoing fade,
  incoming fade and combined fades with relative presets.
- Explicit separation of human choices, machine-verifiable facts, unexecuted
  proposals, saved reconstructions and native results.
- Optional verified editor inspection and bounded saved-correction capture.

Representative decoded picture checks and independently calculated PCM/envelope
checks support source-preview composition. Encoded-preview checks establish that
selected simulation audio reaches the preview movie. Muted playback tests do not
establish human sound-quality approval or native render equivalence.

## Engineering corrections

The campaign fixed history refresh selecting an old row and undoing Next,
late asynchronous completion enabling a newly gated control, insufficient
cached-treatment binding, incomplete single-instance coverage and ambiguous
same-name review scopes.

Recent related work added bounded Windows session-save sharing-conflict retries,
independent scrolling, no-autoplay loading, source-order history, preserved
per-case drafts and source-bound incoming-title contracts. A former opening
locator described the end of an opening animation rather than the later title;
adjacent original frames corrected the proposed picture/audio start. This was
not an applied production edit or proof all title detection is solved.

No-tail cases no longer offer identical Keep/Delete choices. An existing cut
does not imply human approval, and a saved-result review cannot silently approve
a distinct audio-choice record.

## Open findings

### Unexecuted transition versus preview availability

One inspected transition remains an original unedited source join. Recovering
its incoming title-exit evidence enables an explicitly proposed preview; it does
not execute the edit or create human authorization.

### Source geometry versus native render

Independent original decoders identify a final graphic frame followed by a clean
frame. Saved geometry starts at the clean frame, yet a tiny isolated native export
begins with the graphic. This rules out stale live preview as the sole explanation
in that diagnostic. The native clock/decoder cause and a safe general corrective
operation remain unresolved. Simple rounding adjustments did not fix it.

No accepted correction was applied. The diagnostic is not an export of the whole
accepted project, and similar compressed adjacent pictures do not uniquely prove
every source-frame ordinal. It proves unwanted graphic content and the need for
export-level validation.

### Performance and coverage

An earlier redundant timestamp reentry was removed and bounded single-seek
checks passed. In the latest difficult checks, strict verified navigation still
took approximately **77–107 seconds** and five bounded seek candidates per check.
Removing a duplicate command did not solve total latency.

35 historical records remain gated on qualified incoming-opening evidence.
Unsupported mappings, saved envelopes, restoration dependencies and uncertain
artistic choices must stay visible rather than be guessed into successful results.

## What approval means

A preview approval records the exact reviewed source simulation. It does not
apply the treatment in Filmora, certify final native output, authorize similar
future edits or resolve an unrelated review scope. Native audio Apply remains
disabled. Human corrections record observable saved changes without inventing
their reasons.

## Next qualification priorities

1. Resolve and qualify the native rendered-frame discrepancy on disposable work.
2. Recover missing source evidence without weakening reconstruction gates.
3. Improve measured navigation latency while preserving strict verification.
4. Expand repeated review/correction, interruption and supported-restoration tests.
5. Independently qualify native audiovisual output and supported end-to-end plans.

General arbitrary-project editing, unattended artistic execution, universal
recovery and automated configurable encoding remain unfinished. Independent
external beta testing has not occurred and is not open.
