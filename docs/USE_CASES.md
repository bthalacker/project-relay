# Potential Uses for Educators and Video Teams

**Future applications, not currently validated organizational workflows.**
Relay is pre-alpha. It has bounded demonstrations of saved-edit comparison,
guided handoff, authorized editing and limited restoration. The examples below
describe where those foundations might eventually help.

## Long recorded classes and training sessions

An instructor could review candidate pauses or unwanted sections without
searching the entire recording manually. A trainer could keep a meaningful
reflection pause while removing an accidental interruption.

General silence/pausing search, reliable navigation and the complete review flow
still need qualification on this kind of content. A quiet interval is not
automatically unnecessary.

## Tutorials and webinars

Relay could help locate transitions, title sequences and repeated sections,
then let the creator decide what belongs in the final lesson. Repeated material
may be useful reinforcement; an unfamiliar segment may still be unwanted.
Content similarity cannot decide instructional value on its own.

## Collections of training videos

Approved examples might help test reusable rules for recurring openings,
transitions or audio handoffs. Each proposed rule would need independent
examples, negative cases and a defined supported scope. Matching one title or
sound does not authorize changes across an entire course.

## Review after a supported editing batch

An integrated QC queue could guide a coordinator or video editor through affected
areas, play enough picture/audio context, and record accept/defer/correct choices.
Supported selective restoration could make an earlier correction less disruptive
to later work.

A limited restoration has been demonstrated, and a QC queue is prepared; the
complete live correction-and-resume workflow is still experimental. Full rendered
audio/visual QC and broad restoration are not qualified.

## Human control stays central

The intended benefit is less repetitive searching, editing and checking, with
the creator retaining decisions about content, speech, pacing and the final cut.
Relay should surface uncertainty and preserve original material, rather than
silently remove something because it resembles a prior example.

There are no benchmark-based time-saving promises and no established external
education/training pilot is being announced. Outside testing is not open, and
this repository is not a media-submission channel.
